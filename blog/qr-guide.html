QR codes are everywhere — on product packaging, business cards, restaurant menus, and event posters. Generating one used to require an account and a paid plan. Today you can create a fully customised, vector-quality QR code in seconds, directly in your browser, for free.

1. What QR Codes Are Used For

A QR code encodes any string of data into a scannable image. Common uses include linking to a website, sharing Wi-Fi credentials, displaying contact information, directing customers to a payment page, or embedding a plain text message. Because the data is stored in the code itself, QR codes work completely offline — no server needed to decode them.

💡 Tip: For printed materials, always download the SVG version of your QR code. SVG scales to any size without losing sharpness, unlike a small PNG scaled up.

2. Step by Step

  • Enter your content — paste a URL, type text, or enter Wi-Fi details.
  • Choose error correction — higher levels let the code stay scannable even if partly covered or damaged.
  • Customise the style — pick foreground and background colors, or apply a gradient.
  • Add a logo — upload a PNG or SVG to embed in the center of the code.
  • Download — export as SVG for print or PNG for digital use.

3. Styling Your QR Code

A plain black-and-white QR code works but looks generic. Custom colors and a centered logo make the code feel on-brand and more trustworthy to users who are deciding whether to scan it. Key styling rules to follow:

  • Keep sufficient contrast — the foreground must be significantly darker than the background for scanners to read it reliably.
  • Avoid light-on-dark if scanning is critical — some older scanner apps struggle with inverted colors.
  • Keep logo size under 30% of the code area — larger logos obstruct too many data modules.
  • Test before printing — always scan the final code on a real device before putting it on physical materials.

4. Tips for Reliable Scanning

  • Use a minimum size of 2 cm × 2 cm for print. Smaller codes are harder for phone cameras to focus on.
  • Add a quiet zone — leave a white border of at least four modules around the code.
  • Use high error correction (H level) when adding a logo, to compensate for the covered modules.
  • Avoid placing codes on curved surfaces — distortion makes scanning unreliable.
