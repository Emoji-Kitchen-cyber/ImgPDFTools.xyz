The Complete Favicon Guide: Sizes, Formats and How to Make One

A favicon is the tiny icon that appears in the browser tab, bookmarks, history, and on mobile home screens. It is small, but it carries your brand everywhere your site is pinned or shared. This guide covers the sizes and formats you need and how to generate a full set in seconds.

1. What a Favicon Is and Why It Matters

Beyond decoration, the favicon is a trust and recognition signal. A clear, on-brand icon helps users find your tab among dozens of others, makes bookmarks look professional, and gives your progressive web app a real home-screen identity.

Modern browsers, iOS, Android, and PWA manifests all request different icon sizes, so a single 16x16 image is no longer enough. The trick is to start from one clean, high-resolution source and export every size from it.

💡 Tip: Design your icon at 512x512 with bold, simple shapes. Fine detail disappears at 16x16, so keep it readable when shrunk.

2. Sizes and Formats You Need

A complete, future-proof favicon set usually includes:

  • 16x16 and 32x32 ICO — classic browser tab and bookmark icons.
  • 48x48 — helps older Windows and some browsers.
  • 180x180 Apple touch icon — for iOS home screens.
  • 192x192 and 512x512 PNG — for Android and the PWA manifest.
  • SVG (optional) — scales perfectly on high-DPI displays.

ICO offers the widest compatibility, PNG is crisp for modern browsers and manifests, and SVG stays razor-sharp at any size. Shipping all three covers every device.

3. Adding the Favicon to Your Site

Once generated, drop the files in your site root and add a few lines inside the <head>: a link for the ICO, one for the Apple touch icon, and references in your web manifest for the PNG sizes. Then clear your cache — browsers cache favicons aggressively, so a hard refresh is often needed to see the new icon.

Frequently Asked Questions

At minimum a 32x32 and 16x16 ICO, a 180x180 Apple touch icon, and 192x192 plus 512x512 PNGs for Android and PWA manifests. A 48x48 helps older browsers.
